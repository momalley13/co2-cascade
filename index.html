CO2 Cascade Diagnostic

Subcritical R-744 low stage, about -40 to -50 C. Record P and T, then compute.

1 Evaporator superheat

Use PT-2, coil outlet. Not compressor suction.

Low SH: flood-back. High SH: starved coil, warm corners.

2 Subcooling

PT-6 cascade liquid out, then again at PT-8.

Bubbles in the sight glass: subcooling is already gone.

3 Cascade approach

CO2 liquid temperature at PT-6 minus high-stage evaporating temperature from PT-9.

Growing approach: fouled exchanger, oil logging, or a weak high stage. Check PT-10 first.
Heat into the cascade exchanger = room load + low-stage compressor power.

What the readings mean

What you seeCheck before you adjust a setpoint
Slow recovery after a doorCascade approach. Is the high stage taking room load plus compressor power?
Warm corners, cold supplyCoil superheat high, or airflow. Do not use compressor superheat.
Will not go below about -50 CTriple-point wall. Do not defeat the cutout.
Head climbs over daysNon-condensables, fouled cascade or gas cooler, overcharge. Read approach, not just the gauge.
Night spikesDefrost or short-cycle. Align the event log with the probe.
Relief lifted over a weekendCharge is gone. Expect a slow failure to recover.
Oil tripLow mass flow, riser velocity, or cold-suction viscosity. Trend the oil level.
Sight glass bubblingSubcooling gone. Low charge, restriction, or liquid-line pressure drop.

The three formulas

Superheat = coil-outlet T − Tsat at coil-outlet P
Subcooling = Tsat at liquid P − liquid T
Approach = cascade liquid T − high-stage evaporating T

A gas cooler has no subcooling. Above 31 C (88 F) there is no saturation pressure.
